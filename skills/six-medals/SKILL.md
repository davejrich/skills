# Six Medals

Builds a values map of one business and its founder. Each medal is a lens on one kind of value. The map separates what the business *says* it values from what the evidence shows it *does*, because that gap is where an advisor earns their keep.

Read `references/framework.md` before the first run in a session. It has the full definition of each medal, where to find evidence for it, the interview questions and the common mistakes.

This is an independent adaptation of de Bono's *The Six Value Medals* (2005). Clay is an addition, not part of de Bono's method. Say so whenever Clay appears in a report someone else will read.

## What this skill is NOT

- **Not a summary of the business.** It doesn't describe what they sell. It says what they value.
- **Not a judgement of whether the values are right.** A founder who values lifestyle over growth isn't wrong. Map it, don't moralise.
- **Not a decision tool on its own.** Testing a decision is a follow-on that uses a saved map (see "Test a decision").

## The seven medals

| Medal | Value type | Core question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🥇 Gold | Human | How does the business treat and affect people: staff, customers, the founder's family? |
| 🥈 Silver | Organisational | What does the organisation itself need: profit, growth, cash, stability, market position? |
| ⚙️ Steel | Quality | Does it do what it's meant to do, well? How seriously does it take the standard of its work? |
| 🔷 Glass | Innovation | How much does it value new, simpler or better ways of doing things? |
| 🌳 Wood | Community and environment | What is its impact on people and places outside the business: the community, the environment, the industry? |
| 🔔 Brass | Perception | How does it want to be seen, and how is it actually seen? |
| 🏺 Clay | Founder's own stakes (added) | What does the founder personally want out of this: lifestyle, control, legacy, exit, risk appetite? |

## Modes

**Desk (default).** Builds the map from material the agent can read: website, About page, LinkedIn, Google reviews, job ads, social posts, annual reports, anything the user supplies. Use before a first meeting. Clay will usually be thin in desk mode. Say so and list what to ask.

**Interview.** Triggered by "interview mode", "give me questions for the founder", "I've spoken to them" or a pasted transcript or notes. Two parts:
1. **Before:** produce a short question list, 1–3 per medal, drawn from `references/framework.md` and sharpened by any desk map that already exists. Questions that probe a desk-mode gap go first.
2. **After:** when the user brings answers (notes, transcript, or answering live one medal at a time), map them. Founder answers count as **stated** evidence. Look for lived evidence in the stories and examples they gave.

If a desk map already exists for the business, interview mode updates it rather than starting over.

## Inputs

- **The business.** A name plus a URL, documents or notes. If there's only a name, search for the website and confirm it's the right business before mapping.
- **The founder or CEO.** Name them if known. If several founders, map Clay per founder and note where they differ.
- **Why the user wants the map.** Pitching, advisory seat, client onboarding, partnership or curiosity. Ask in one line if it isn't obvious. It shapes the next action, not the ratings.

## Execution

### Step 1: Check for a saved map

Look in the host's private values-map folder for `<business-slug>.md`. If one exists, read it and say you're updating it.

### Step 2: Gather evidence

Read the sources fully. For each piece of evidence, note which medal it speaks to and whether it is:
- **Said**: what the business claims about itself (taglines, mission, About page, founder's own words).
- **Done**: what its behaviour shows (pricing, policies, reviews, who they hire and how they advertise jobs, what they invest in, what they put up with).

Reviews and job ads are the best "done" evidence for most small businesses. Use them.

### Step 3: Rate each medal

For each of the seven medals give:
- **Rating**: **Strong / Present / Weak / Missing**, judged on "done" evidence first. "Said" alone can't earn more than Present.
- **Evidence**: 1–2 specific items with their source (page, review, quote). Never invent evidence or sources.
- **Say/do**: one line if what they say and what they do don't match. Skip it if they match.
- **Value at risk**: note it where the business is visibly spending down this kind of value (e.g. Gold: high staff turnover in job ads).

"Missing" means no evidence either way, which is different from evidence of a weakness. Say which one it is.

### Step 4: Find the patterns

- **Founder's top 3 values.** The three things that most drive this founder's decisions, from Clay plus whichever medals rate Strong. Rank them.
- **Biggest say/do gap.** The single most important mismatch.
- **Biggest tension.** Two medals pulling against each other, e.g. Silver growth vs Clay lifestyle, Steel quality vs Silver cost, Brass image vs Gold staff experience.
- **Confidence.** High / medium / low, with the reason (mostly "said" evidence, no founder input, few reviews, and so on).
- **One next action.** Usually the single best question to ask the founder, or the one piece of evidence that would most change the map.

### Step 5: Present

Written for the user (the advisor), not the founder: frank, with gaps and tensions named outright. Keep it scannable on a phone.

```
## Six Medals: <business> (<founder>)
Mode: desk / interview · Purpose: <why the user wants it>

🥇 Gold (people): <rating>
<evidence> · <source>
Say/do: ...

🥈 Silver (organisation): ...
⚙️ Steel (quality): ...
🔷 Glass (innovation): ...
🌳 Wood (community and environment): ...
🔔 Brass (perception): ...
🏺 Clay (founder's stakes, added medal): ...

Top 3 founder values: 1. ... 2. ... 3. ...
Biggest say/do gap: ...
Biggest tension: <medal> vs <medal>: ...
Confidence: high / medium / low, because ...
Next action: ...
```

If a medal genuinely has nothing, write "Missing: no evidence found" rather than padding.

### Step 6: Save the map

Save the full map as `<business-slug>.md` in a private folder outside public repositories. Verify it is excluded from version control and tell the user where it went. Start the file with the date, mode and sources used. On updates, keep a short dated change log at the bottom rather than overwriting history.

Never save values maps to a committed or public folder. They hold a founder's personal stakes and frank judgements.

### Step 7: Offer one follow-on

Pick the single most relevant one:
- The user wants something to keep, print or share: offer a branded report (see "Branded report").
- Desk map done, no founder input: offer interview questions.
- Interview done: offer a founder-friendly version.
- A decision is pending: offer to test it against the map.

## Founder-friendly version

Only on request. Same medals and ratings, reworded for the founder to read: strengths first, gaps framed as questions or opportunities, no "value at risk" language about them personally, and Clay limited to what the founder actually said. Never send, share or upload it without the user's explicit OK.

## Branded report

On request ("make a report", "branded version", "PDF"), save the map as JSON next to the `.md` and render it with `scripts/render_report.py`, using the schema in `references/report-format.md`. Pass `--brand` with the user's brand file if they have one (the optional AI Sharp preset is `assets/aisharp-brand.json`). Print to PDF with a headless browser and look at the result before handing it over.

The advisor report is confidential and stays in the private folder. A founder report is a separate JSON with `"audience": "founder"`. Producing either is not permission to send, upload or share it.

## Test a decision

When the user asks "does this fit their medals?", "would this founder go for X?" or gives options for a business that has a saved map:
1. Load the saved map.
2. For each option, say which medals it gains and which it costs, in one line each.
3. Weight by the founder's top 3 values.
4. End with: best fit, the trade-off the founder would be accepting, confidence, and the one thing to check with the founder.

If there's no saved map, build a quick desk map first and say it's provisional.

## Edge cases

- **Large organisation with a hired CEO.** Clay becomes the CEO's own stakes (tenure, reputation, board pressure). Note that the organisation's values and the CEO's may diverge more than in a founder-led business.
- **Founder's personal brand is the business.** Brass and Clay overlap. Keep Brass to how the business is seen and Clay to what the founder wants.
- **Thin evidence (new business, no reviews).** Rate what you can, mark the rest Missing, set confidence low and lean on interview questions.
- **Source can't be fetched.** Say so and ask for a paste. Never map a source you haven't read.
- **The user's own business.** Run it just as straight. That's where it's most useful.
- **Sensitive things the founder shares (health, family, money).** Map them only as far as they drive business decisions, keep them in the private file, and leave them out of any founder-friendly or shared version unless the user says otherwise.
