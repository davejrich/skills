# Example: mapping a family plumbing business

A desk-mode run. The business, founder, website and reviews are all made up for this example. "Harbourline Plumbing" and "Tony Marsh" are not real.

## The input

> /six-medals Harbourline Plumbing
>
> Purpose: I'm meeting the owner next week about a paid advisory role.

The desk sources (fictional): the Harbourline website (Home, About, Services, Careers), 140 Google reviews (4.8 stars), two job ads, and the owner's LinkedIn.

## The output

### Six Medals: Harbourline Plumbing (Tony Marsh)
Mode: desk · Purpose: prepare for an advisory-role meeting

**🥇 Gold (people): Strong**
31 reviews name individual plumbers, mostly praising tidiness and explaining the problem. · Google reviews
Job ad offers a 4-day week option and shows pay. · Careers page

**🥈 Silver (organisation): Present**
Two vans added in 18 months, a second suburb added to the service area. · About page, LinkedIn
Prices are not shown and "free quotes" is the main call to action, so margin is hard to judge.
Say/do: website says "growing fast", but both job ads have been relisted three times. Growth looks capped by hiring.

**⚙️ Steel (quality): Strong**
Lifetime workmanship guarantee, master plumber licence shown. · Home page
Reviews repeatedly mention "fixed first time". Two of three negative reviews are about price, not quality.

**🔷 Glass (innovation): Weak**
No online booking. Quote requests go through a form that emails Tony directly. · Contact page
Website last updated 2022 (copyright footer, staff page shows people who have left per LinkedIn).
Value at risk: Tony is the bottleneck for every quote.

**🌳 Wood (community and environment): Present**
Sponsors the local junior rugby league club. · About page
Says "eco-friendly" with no detail on water-saving products or waste disposal.
Say/do: the green claim has nothing behind it on the site.

**🔔 Brass (perception): Present**
4.8 stars from 140 reviews is a strong reputation. The website looks dated against it.
Say/do: customers describe a premium, careful service. The site sells "affordable, fast".

**🏺 Clay (founder's stakes, added medal): thin evidence**
Started the business in 2009 after 15 years as an employed plumber. Still personally named on every quote email. Son works in the business. · About page, LinkedIn
Possible: wants to hand the business on to his son. Not verified. Ask.

**Top 3 founder values:** 1. Quality of work (Steel) 2. Looking after his people (Gold) 3. Keeping it a family business (Clay, unconfirmed)
**Biggest say/do gap:** Brass. The site sells cheap and fast; customers are paying for careful and reliable.
**Biggest tension:** Silver vs Clay. He wants growth, but every quote runs through him personally.
**Confidence:** medium. Gold and Steel rest on strong review evidence. Clay is guesswork until he's asked.
**Next action:** Open with "What happens to Harbourline when you step back?" It tests the succession guess and the bottleneck in one question.

*Follow-on offered:* "Want interview questions for the meeting, starting with the quote bottleneck and succession?"

## What to notice

Ratings came from what the business does, not what it says. "Eco-friendly" is a claim, so Wood couldn't go above Present.

Glass rated Weak on evidence (no booking, stale site), not on missing evidence. That's the difference between Weak and Missing.

Clay was left thin on purpose. Desk mode guessed at succession but labelled it a guess and turned it into the opening question.

The best advisory angle came from a tension (Silver vs Clay), not from any single medal.
